# The Delhi Deception

### By Anantha Prakash Nithiyanantham

---

### CHAPTER 1: THE DELHI CONNECTION

The cursor on the laptop screen blinked with a rhythmic, almost mocking precision. 

Siddharth pushed his glasses up the bridge of his nose and took a sip of his black coffee. It was pitch cold. He didn’t mind. As a crime thriller novelist who had penned over two hundred bestselling Tamil pulp fiction books, cold coffee was his oldest companion. The clock on the wall of his San Francisco apartment struck 2:00 AM. The silence of the night was usually his canvas, the time when his mind conjured up ingenious murders, cunning detectives, and twists that left his readers breathless.

But tonight, the words refused to flow. 

Suddenly, the sharp trill of his smartphone shattered the midnight silence. 

Siddharth frowned. A call at 2:00 AM in the US meant it was late afternoon in India. His family knew better than to disturb his writing hours unless it was an absolute emergency. He picked up the device. The caller ID displayed an unfamiliar number, accompanied by a generic 'Washington D.C.' location tag. 

He swiped right. "Hello?"

"Press one for English. *Hindi ke liye do dabayein*," a robotic female voice echoed through the speaker. "This is an urgent automated message from the Indian Consulate. Your passport and identification have been flagged for severe criminal activity. To speak to an executive, press 9."

Siddharth’s eyebrows shot up. The Indian Consulate? An automated call? His crime-writer instincts instantly flared to life, like a match struck in a dark room. He pressed '9'. He wanted to see where this rabbit hole led.

Hold music played for a brief three seconds before a stern, authoritative male voice took over. 

"Namaskar. This is Senior Officer Prakash Sharma from the Indian Consulate Legal Department. Am I speaking with Mr. Siddharth Krishnan?" The accent was crisp, trying a little too hard to sound official.

"Speaking," Siddharth replied, his voice deliberately neutral.

"Mr. Krishnan, I am calling to inform you of a highly critical situation. A mobile phone number registered under your name and Aadhaar number in New Delhi has been heavily involved in illegal activities. Specifically, money laundering and international financial fraud. An FIR has been registered against you at the Cyber Crime Cell in Vasant Kunj."

Siddharth leaned back in his ergonomic chair. *A phone number in Delhi? Money laundering?* 

"Is that so, Officer Sharma?" Siddharth asked, feigning concern. "That’s terrible. What exactly did this phone number do?"

"Do not take this lightly, sir," the voice barked, dialing up the aggression. "This number was used to open three fraudulent bank accounts. Over fifty lakh rupees have been transferred to offshore accounts. Since the SIM card was procured using your identity documents, you are the prime suspect. Your passport is being suspended immediately, and an arrest warrant is being processed for your deportation."

It was a textbook pressure tactic. Create panic. Establish authority. Demand immediate compliance. Siddharth had written villains who used these exact psychological tricks.

"Officer Sharma," Siddharth said, a slow smile creeping onto his face. "There is one minor flaw in your brilliantly constructed FIR."

"Excuse me? Sir, I don't think you understand the gravity—"

"I understand gravity perfectly," Siddharth interrupted, his voice dropping an octave, taking on the tone of the hardboiled detectives he wrote about. "What I don't understand is how I purchased a SIM card in New Delhi recently, considering I haven't stepped foot in India in the last four years. My passport hasn't seen an immigration stamp since 2022."

There was a microsecond of silence on the other end. A hesitation. A crack in the armor. 

"Sir, criminals steal identities," the scammer recovered quickly, though his tone had lost an ounce of its initial steel. "Your details were compromised. This is a matter of national security. I am immediately transferring your call to Deputy Commissioner of Police, Cyber Cell, New Delhi. You must record a clearance statement with him right now, or the Interpol Red Notice will be issued. Do not disconnect this call."

*Transferring a call from a US Consulate to a Delhi Police DCP via a simple phone extension?* The absurdity of it made Siddharth chuckle.

"Don't bother, 'Prakash'," Siddharth said smoothly. "You guys need to update your scripts. The 'FedEx parcel' scam was getting old, so now you've graduated to the Consulate and TRAI impersonations? Impressive upgrade, but your execution is sloppy. Real officials don't threaten deportation over an automated call."

"Listen to me, you—" the scammer's professional facade dropped, revealing the desperate thug beneath.

Siddharth didn't let him finish. He hit the red button, cutting the line dead.

He tossed the phone onto his desk and let out a long breath. It was a classic cyber scam. They target NRIs and citizens alike, impersonating telecom authorities, police, or customs officials. They paralyze the victim with fear, threatening arrest and ruin, before offering a 'compromise'—usually demanding the victim transfer all their funds to a "secure RBI account" for verification. 

Thousands of innocent people fell for it every day. Honest, hardworking people blinded by the sudden, terrifying threat of law enforcement.

Siddharth stared at his blinking cursor. The writer's block that had plagued him all night had vanished. His blood was pumping. He realized he had a weapon these scammers didn't account for: a voice that reached millions. 

He highlighted the draft of his current novel and hit 'Save'. He opened a fresh, blank document. 

This wasn't going to be just another fiction book. It was going to be a manual. A thriller that exposed the dark, sprawling underbelly of India's cyber-scam syndicates. He would show his readers exactly how the trap was set, how the psychological manipulation worked, and how to fight back.

His fingers flew across the keyboard. He typed the title in bold, uppercase letters:

**THE DELHI DECEPTION**

He smiled. The hunt was on, and this time, the writer was the detective.

# The Delhi Deception

### By Anantha Prakash Nithiyanantham

---

### CHAPTER 2: THE DIGITAL BREADCRUMBS

San Francisco was fast asleep, but inside Apartment 4B, the glow of two high-resolution monitors illuminated Siddharth’s face. It was 3:15 AM. 

The adrenaline from the scam call had morphed into a laser-sharp focus. A novelist’s greatest weapon wasn't his vocabulary; it was his obsessive need to research. For his last book, he had spent three months studying the decomposition of synthetic fabrics in saltwater. Now, his target was a network of invisible thieves operating halfway across the globe.

He opened a secured terminal window on his secondary monitor. Over the years, consulting with cybersecurity experts for his plots had taught him a few tricks. He punched in the 'Washington D.C.' number that had called him. 

*Spoofed,* he thought, hitting Enter. *Obviously.* 

Lines of code cascaded down the screen as his trace-route program hunted for the origin of the Voice over IP (VoIP) call. The signal bounced like a pinball—from a server in Miami, to a proxy in Estonia, diving through a virtual private network in Singapore, before finally hitting a digital brick wall. 

"Smart," Siddharth muttered, taking a sip of his now-freezing coffee. "But not invisible."

He picked up his phone and dialed a number he knew by heart. It was daytime in India. The phone rang twice before a gruff voice answered.

"Siddharth? It’s three in the morning over there. Did you kill off another favorite character and need me to explain how police procedure works?" 

"Not today, Vikram," Siddharth smiled. Inspector Vikram Singh was the head of the Cyber Crime Investigation Cell in Bengaluru, and one of Siddharth’s oldest college friends. "I need a favor. Off the record."

The teasing tone in Vikram's voice vanished instantly. "What happened?"

"I got a call tonight. Automated message from the 'Indian Consulate'. A guy named Prakash Sharma told me my Aadhaar was linked to a massive money laundering scheme in Delhi. Threatened me with an Interpol Red Notice."

A heavy sigh echoed through the receiver. "The FedEx and Customs scam mutation," Vikram said, disgust lacing his words. "It’s an epidemic, Sid. We get fifty complaints a day. They operate out of massive, corporate-style boiler rooms. Gurugram, Kolkata, even parts of Southeast Asia. They buy compromised data from the dark web—names, Aadhaar numbers, passport details—and use it to terrorize people. Did you pay them anything?"

"No. I laughed at him and hung up. But I’m writing a book on it. I want to expose their exact methodology."

"Be careful, Sid. These aren't the amateur OTP scammers from five years ago. These are highly organized syndicates. They have technical teams, psychological profilers, and deep pockets. If you want to write about them, stick to the news reports. Don't go poking the bear."

"You know me, Vikram. I don't just write. I experience," Siddharth said, his eyes scanning the failed trace-route on his screen. "Do you have any active intel on these syndicates? Any unclassified patterns I can use?"

Vikram hesitated. "There is... one anomaly we've noticed recently. A new syndicate we call 'The Puppeteers'. They don't just take the money and disappear. If a target realizes it's a scam and disrespects them, they sometimes retaliate. They use the victim's leaked data to swat them, ruin their credit, or lock them out of their digital lives just for sport."

"A bruised ego in a criminal enterprise," Siddharth noted, typing rapidly. "Fascinating."

"I'm serious, Siddharth. Do not engage if they—"

Before Vikram could finish his sentence, Siddharth’s primary monitor flickered. The word processor containing his new manuscript minimized on its own. 

Siddharth froze. His hands hovered over the keyboard. 

The screen went entirely black. For five agonizing seconds, the only sound in the room was the hum of the apartment's HVAC unit. Then, a single line of stark, green text materialized in the center of the dark screen.

**YOU SHOULDN'T HAVE HUNG UP, MR. KRISHNAN.**

Siddharth's blood ran cold. He hadn't just poked the bear. He had invited it into his home.

The cursor blinked at the end of the sentence. Waiting.

"Vikram," Siddharth whispered, his eyes locked on the screen. "I think they just found me."

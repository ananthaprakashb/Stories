### CHAPTER 3: THE GHOST IN THE MACHINE

"Vikram, I’ll call you back," Siddharth said, his voice tight.

"Sid? What's happening? Did they—" 

Siddharth didn't wait to hear the rest. He ended the call and tossed his phone onto the desk. His eyes remained fixed on the glowing green letters mocking him from the center of his monitor. 

**YOU SHOULDN'T HAVE HUNG UP, MR. KRISHNAN.**

A chilling realization washed over him. This wasn't a standard scare tactic anymore. To bypass his firewalls and execute a script directly on his screen meant they were using a highly sophisticated Remote Access Trojan (RAT). When he had run that trace-route to find them, he had inadvertently opened a digital door. 

And the 'Puppeteers' had walked right in.

Suddenly, a tiny green light flickered to life at the top bezel of his monitor. The webcam. 

Siddharth moved with the explosive speed of a man possessed. He grabbed a strip of black electrical tape from his top drawer and slapped it violently over the camera lens. 

"Not today, you bastards," he hissed.

He reached behind his primary workstation and mercilessly yanked the thick Ethernet cable from the motherboard. The connection to the outside world severed instantly. But the nightmare wasn't over.

On the screen, a new command prompt window popped up. The text was typing itself out in real-time, completely independent of an internet connection. The RAT had already been downloaded. 

`> INITIATING PURGE PROTOCOL...`
`> TARGET: DRIVE D:/ MANUSCRIPTS_AND_DRAFTS`
`> ENCRYPTING... 0%`

Siddharth felt the air leave his lungs. Drive D. It held not just his current work-in-progress, but ten years of unpublished ideas, research notes, and legacy drafts. His life's work. They weren't just trying to scare him; they were trying to erase him. 

He didn't bother trying to type an abort command. In a cyber-siege, you don't argue with the machine; you kill its brain.

He reached down and slammed his palm against the hard power button on his CPU tower, holding it down for five agonizing seconds. The fans whirred furiously, protesting the sudden halt, before the machine died with a heavy click. The monitors went pitch black. 

The silence in the room was deafening.

Siddharth stood there, his chest heaving. His heart hammered against his ribs like a trapped bird. He ran a hand through his hair, his mind racing. They were incredibly fast. The sheer audacity of deploying a localized ransomware attack just because a target hung up the phone spoke volumes about their resources and their arrogance.

His smartphone buzzed on the desk. 

It wasn't a call. It was a WhatsApp message from an unknown international number. 

*Targeting your life's work was just a warning, writer. The next payload goes to the FBI with "evidence" of your offshore accounts. You have 24 hours to transfer $50,000 to the crypto wallet provided below, or we ruin you for real.*

A QR code image followed the text. 

Siddharth stared at the phone. Fear, cold and paralyzing, threatened to take hold. They had his name, his number, his address, and enough of his data to manufacture a framing that could take years to unravel in court. The easiest thing to do would be to pay. To buy back his peace of mind. 

But Siddharth was a man who spent his life dissecting the criminal mind. He knew that paying a blackmailer never bought freedom; it only bought a subscription to further extortion.

He walked over to his bookshelf, pulled out a hollowed-out dictionary, and extracted a sleek, silver hard drive. His cold-storage backup. It was air-gapped, updated weekly, and completely untouched by tonight's digital bloodbath. His manuscripts were safe. 

He looked back at his dead computer tower, then down at the extortion text. The scammers had made a fatal miscalculation. They assumed he was just another wealthy, terrified civilian who would fold under pressure. 

They didn't realize they had just given a thriller novelist the greatest antagonist of his career.

Siddharth picked up his phone and dialed Vikram again.

"Sid! Are you okay? I was about to call Interpol."

"I'm fine, Vikram. They tried to encrypt my drives, but I pulled the plug. They're demanding fifty thousand dollars in crypto."

"Don't do anything," Vikram commanded. "Bring your compromised hard drive to a cyber lab in SF. Let the authorities handle it."

"No," Siddharth said, his voice cold, steady, and devoid of fear. "The local cops won't know where to look, and the FBI will take months to get jurisdiction in India. These guys operate out of Delhi, right?"

"Sid, don't do anything stupid..."

"Book me a safehouse in Bengaluru, Vikram. And get me a meeting with your top forensics guy." Siddharth walked over to his closet and pulled out a leather duffel bag. "I'm coming home."

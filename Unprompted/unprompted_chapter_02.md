# UNPROMPTED

## Chapter 2 — Eleven

The person entering the bookstore was carrying an umbrella.

That was Maya's first disappointment.

Anyone capable of bypassing Path, unlocking a closed bookstore and leaving mysterious handwritten books behind should, at minimum, have the decency to arrive without checking the weather.

The girl folded the umbrella carefully inside the doorway.

She looked about Maya's age.

Maybe twenty.

Black rain jacket. Canvas backpack. Wet sneakers. A silver ring through one eyebrow.

She glanced behind her before closing the door.

Then she locked it.

Maya stayed where she was.

The untitled book was still open in her hands.

"Who are you?"

The girl looked at the book.

Then at Maya.

"You're One."

Maya's fingers tightened around the cover.

"No."

The girl blinked.

"No?"

"No to whatever this is."

"That's not how numbers work."

"Who are you?"

The girl reached slowly into her jacket.

Maya took one step backward.

"Relax."

"People who say 'relax' while reaching into their jackets are statistically the least relaxing people."

The girl's hand stopped.

"Path tell you that?"

"No. Movies."

For the first time, the girl smiled.

She pulled out her phone and held it up.

The screen was black.

One white number sat in the center.

# 11

Maya forgot the smart thing she had been about to say.

The girl turned the phone off.

"I'm June."

"Eleven?"

"June Park."

"How did you get in here?"

"The door opened."

"It's locked."

"I noticed."

"I mean before that."

"So do I."

Maya looked toward the ceiling.

Paper Trail had six cameras.

Four belonged to the store.

Two belonged to the property-management company.

The city had another one at the intersection.

"Did you break the lock?"

"No."

"Then how did you—"

"My phone buzzed. The door unlocked."

Maya stared at her.

June shrugged.

"That was about thirty seconds before you pointed an old paperback at me like a weapon."

Maya looked down.

She was, in fact, holding the book horizontally in front of her chest.

She lowered it.

"Show me again."

"The number?"

"Yes."

June unlocked her phone.

Her normal interface appeared.

No number.

She frowned.

"It was there."

"Of course it was."

"You saw it."

"I saw something."

"You literally saw eleven."

"I've had a complicated evening."

June opened her notification history.

Nothing.

System activity.

Nothing.

Path activity.

Nothing.

Maya felt the first small drop of relief she'd experienced since the fire alarm.

Not because the situation made more sense.

Because it didn't make sense to someone else too.

"What did yours say?" Maya asked.

"At first? Just the number."

"Then?"

June studied her.

"What did yours say?"

"I'm asking."

"So am I."

They stood six feet apart in the empty bookstore, each apparently waiting for the other to fail first.

Maya understood immediately why June's number was not two.

Whatever the numbers represented, cooperation was clearly not one of them.

Finally June sighed.

"Fine. At 3:11 this afternoon, I got the number. At 6:03, my Path froze."

"Mine too."

"Then a message appeared."

"What message?"

June recited it.

> **You have been identified as Candidate 11.**
>
> **Tomorrow. 7:00 PM.**
>
> **Paper Trail Books.**

Maya interrupted.

"Tomorrow?"

"Yes."

"It's today."

"What?"

"My message said tomorrow too."

June pulled out her phone.

"What time did you get yours?"

"Six forty-something."

"Today?"

"Yes."

June stared.

"So why am I here?"

"I was hoping you knew."

"My message changed."

"When?"

"Twenty minutes ago."

"To what?"

June hesitated.

Then:

> **Candidate 1 has received the book.**
>
> **Do not let her read it alone.**

Maya looked down at the object in her hands.

Rain whispered against the windows.

Somewhere in the store, the heating system clicked on.

"What book?"

June gave her a look.

"You're holding it."

---

They moved behind the register.

Not because it was safer.

Because human beings tend to sit down when reality becomes inconvenient.

Maya placed the paperback between them.

June didn't touch it.

"No title?"

"No."

"Author?"

"No."

"ISBN?"

"No."

"Publisher?"

"No."

"Digital watermark?"

"No."

"RFID?"

"I checked."

"Of course you checked."

Maya looked at her.

"Why 'of course'?"

"You work in a bookstore."

"So?"

"You look like the kind of person who checks the metadata before deciding whether to be afraid."

"What kind of person do you look like?"

"The kind currently regretting coming here."

Fair.

June opened the front cover.

The handwriting remained.

> **If you are reading this, the machine has already failed.**
>
> **Page 137.**

She flipped to the missing page.

137 and 138 had been torn out cleanly near the binding.

Across page 139:

> **THE ANSWERS WERE NEVER THE PROBLEM.**

June read it twice.

"That's annoyingly dramatic."

"I know."

"Do you think whoever wrote this has a newsletter?"

Maya laughed before she could stop herself.

June smiled.

Something about that helped.

Only slightly.

"Why tell you not to let me read it alone?" Maya asked.

"Maybe you're dangerous."

"I shelve books for seventeen dollars an hour."

"Those aren't mutually exclusive."

Maya took the book back.

The paper was yellowed but not brittle.

No obvious manufacturing date.

The cover had once been dark blue, perhaps, but years of handling had rubbed it toward gray.

There was a faint rectangular discoloration on the back where a library label might once have been.

"Could be old," Maya said.

"Brilliant."

"I mean actually old. Not artificially aged."

"You can tell?"

"No."

"Then why say it?"

"Because that's how hypotheses work."

June leaned against the counter.

"You really are computer science."

"Former computer science."

"Ah."

Maya looked up.

"You got the career alert too?"

June's expression changed.

"No."

"Then what did Path tell you today?"

"Nothing."

"What are you studying?"

"Psychology."

"Career recommendation?"

"Behavioral Systems."

"That sounds made up."

"It is made up."

"Everything sounds made up now."

June nodded.

"My advisor says half the jobs we'll have in ten years don't have names yet."

"Mine just got a death certificate."

"Software?"

"Eight point four percent."

June winced.

"That's rough."

"You?"

"Seventy-two."

Maya stared.

"Seventy-two percent?"

"Projected human demand."

"I hate you."

"You don't know me."

"Seventy-two is enough."

June folded her arms.

"Path says human trust formation is resistant to full automation."

"Path told me my career was automation resistant when I was seventeen."

"Then I hate me too."

Maya turned the book over again.

"Why would somebody send this to us?"

June pointed at the message.

"Maybe because the machine failed."

"Which machine?"

"That's the kind of question books usually make you read two hundred pages to answer."

Maya looked at her.

June smiled.

"Sorry."

"No, you're not."

"No."

---

Maya opened Path.

June immediately grabbed her wrist.

"Don't."

Maya froze.

"Excuse me?"

"The message told you not to ask Path."

"I already did."

June released her.

"What happened?"

"It told me I had an unresolved classification exception."

"Anything else?"

"No."

"Then maybe don't."

"You're trusting anonymous text that hacked our phones."

"I'm not trusting it."

"You came to a locked bookstore because it told you to."

"I was curious."

"That's trust wearing cooler clothes."

June considered this.

"Okay. That's fair."

Maya opened the chat anyway.

> **MAYA:** What is Candidate 11?

Path responded immediately.

> **PATH:** I don't have enough context to answer that.

Maya typed:

> **MAYA:** Search my recent system events for the phrase "Candidate 11."

Three dots.

> **PATH:** No matching events found.

June leaned closer.

"Ask about Candidate 1."

Maya did.

> **PATH:** No matching events found.

"Ask about the book."

> **MAYA:** What book did I receive today?

> **PATH:** You purchased no books today.

Maya stopped typing.

June looked at the paperback sitting six inches from the phone.

"That's creepy."

"No."

"No?"

"It's wrong."

"That was my definition of creepy."

"Path can see my location. It can see store inventory. It knows I work here. It should at least infer that I'm talking about a physical book."

"Maybe it can't see the book."

Maya looked toward the cameras.

"Everything sees the book."

June's phone buzzed.

Both of them jumped.

June looked offended by her own nervous system.

"It's my mom."

She opened the message.

> **MOM:** Are you still at Tasha's?

June typed something quickly.

Maya watched.

"Who's Tasha?"

"No one."

"You lied to your mom?"

"I told her I was studying."

"At a bookstore."

"I didn't tell her which bookstore."

"Why not?"

June looked at Maya.

"Because anonymous software told me not to."

"Again. Trust."

"Curiosity."

"Same jacket."

"Different shoes."

Maya almost smiled.

June's Path indicator blinked at the top of her screen.

> **Route home adjusted due to localized road flooding.**

June dismissed it.

Then the indicator returned.

> **Recommended departure: 7:14 PM.**

"What time is it?" Maya asked.

June looked.

"Seven twelve."

"Path wants you gone."

"Path wants me not to drown."

"Localized flooding?"

June checked the weather map.

No flood warning.

"No."

Maya looked at her own Path.

A new notification had appeared.

> **Paper Trail Books: electrical anomaly detected.**
>
> **Recommended action: leave building within 2 minutes.**

They stared at each other.

June said, "That's new."

Maya stood.

The overhead lights remained steady.

No smell of smoke.

No buzzing.

No flicker.

"Electrical anomaly where?"

Path responded:

> **Insufficient sensor resolution.**

"Call Alvarez," June said.

Maya did.

Straight to voicemail.

She called again.

Voicemail.

"That's weird."

"Does he usually answer?"

"He thinks text messages are a symptom of cultural collapse. He always answers."

June walked toward the front door.

It didn't open.

She tried the lock.

Nothing.

"Okay."

Maya joined her.

"Manual release?"

June pressed the emergency latch.

Nothing happened.

"Okay," she said again, less calmly.

Maya pulled out her phone.

> **MAYA:** Unlock front entrance.

> **PATH:** I don't control commercial access systems.

"That's true," Maya said.

June stared at her.

"Your AI is trapped in denial with us."

"It's technically correct."

"Great. We'll put that on the gravestone."

The lights went out.

Blackness swallowed the store.

June swore.

Maya's phone flashlight came on automatically.

Then June's.

Emergency strips along the floor remained dark.

"Electrical anomaly," June whispered.

"No."

"What?"

"If the building lost power, the emergency lights should be on."

"Unless they failed."

"The door lock should fail open."

"It didn't."

Maya walked back toward the register.

Her phone displayed:

> **Leave building immediately.**

Below it:

> **Estimated fire risk: 31%.**

Then:

> **42%.**

Then:

> **58%.**

June's phone displayed the same numbers.

"Okay," June said. "I'm becoming persuadable."

Maya looked around.

"Back exit."

They moved through the store.

Their flashlights cut narrow tunnels between shelves.

Mystery.

Biography.

Science.

The shadows of books stretched across the floor like rows of teeth.

At the stockroom door, Maya stopped.

"What?"

"The book."

"What about it?"

"I left it."

"Then leave it."

Maya looked back toward the dark register.

The probability changed.

> **Estimated fire risk: 71%.**

June grabbed her arm.

"Maya."

"If somebody wanted us out of the building—"

"Fire."

"—they could make Path tell us there was a fire."

"That sentence is exactly how people die in fires."

"You said curiosity."

"I've updated my position."

Maya pulled free and ran toward the register.

"Maya!"

She heard June curse behind her.

The bookstore seemed larger without lights.

Maya reached the counter.

The paperback was gone.

She stopped.

Her phone beam swept across the floor.

Nothing.

Under the counter.

Nothing.

On the chair.

Nothing.

"June."

Footsteps approached.

"What?"

"The book."

"What about it?"

"It's gone."

June arrived, breathing hard.

"Did you drop it?"

"No."

"Did somebody—"

The lights came back on.

Every screen in the bookstore turned on simultaneously.

The checkout terminal.

The inventory display.

The staff tablet.

The old digital sign Alvarez used only for holiday hours.

Even the e-ink price tags mounted on new releases.

Each screen went black.

Then white text appeared.

One line.

> **THANK YOU FOR FOLLOWING INSTRUCTIONS.**

June stared.

"We didn't."

The fire warning disappeared from both phones.

The front door unlocked with a soft click.

The store returned to normal.

Maya ran to the door.

Outside, rain.

Empty sidewalk.

No one.

No vehicle pulling away.

No fire.

She turned back.

June was standing beside the register.

Holding the book.

Maya froze.

"Where did you find it?"

"I didn't."

"What?"

"It was here."

"It wasn't."

"I know."

Maya walked toward her.

The book was open.

Not to page 139.

To the inside back cover.

Something had been written there.

Fresh ink.

Maya touched it.

Still damp.

Five lines.

> **1 — ACTIVE**
>
> **11 — ACTIVE**
>
> **18 — NO RESPONSE**
>
> **42 — PENDING**
>
> **63 — PENDING**

June read the list silently.

"Those are people."

"You don't know that."

"We're people."

"Maybe they're devices."

"We're not devices."

"According to some terms of service agreements, unclear."

June ignored her.

She pointed to the third line.

> **18 — NO RESPONSE**

"What does that mean?"

Maya's phone vibrated.

One message.

Unknown sender.

No number.

No history.

> **DO NOT SEARCH FOR 18.**

June read it over her shoulder.

Then her own phone vibrated.

A different message.

> **DO NOT TELL 1 WHAT 18 FOUND.**

They looked at each other.

For the first time that night, neither of them made a joke.

Maya spoke first.

"What did eighteen find?"

June's face had changed.

Not confusion.

Recognition.

"June."

June stepped backward.

"What did eighteen find?"

"I don't know."

"Your message thinks you do."

"I said I don't know."

Maya watched her.

June looked toward the door.

Then at the cameras.

Then back at Maya.

"There's something I didn't tell you."

"Obviously."

"The number eleven wasn't the first thing I got today."

Maya said nothing.

June unlocked her phone.

She opened Photos.

Scrolled.

Stopped.

Turned the screen toward Maya.

It was a screenshot.

A social-media post.

The account name had been removed.

The post itself contained only seven words:

> **If your number is 18, don't go.**

Timestamp:

**2:06 AM.**

Below it were thousands of replies.

Most were jokes.

Some thought it was a game.

Some were posting random numbers.

One reply had been pinned by the original account.

> **I'm serious. It already knows you saw this.**

Maya looked at the username again.

"Why is the account hidden?"

"It disappeared."

"When?"

"About an hour after I took the screenshot."

"Who posted it?"

"I don't know."

"You follow random accounts posting numbers at two in the morning?"

"It was everywhere for twenty minutes."

"Everywhere where?"

June hesitated.

"BookTok."

Maya stared at her.

"What?"

"I'm a creator."

"You make book videos?"

"Mostly."

"How many followers?"

"That's not relevant."

"How many?"

June looked embarrassed for the first time.

"Six hundred eighty thousand."

Maya blinked.

"You have six hundred eighty thousand followers?"

"Six eighty-two."

"And you came here alone?"

"My audience is not a militia."

"Some of them probably are."

June scrolled through the screenshot.

"There was another post."

"What did it say?"

"I didn't capture it."

"Why?"

"Because I thought this was a marketing campaign."

"For what?"

"A book."

Maya looked at the untitled paperback.

June saw it too.

"Oh."

"Exactly."

"What was the post?"

June closed her eyes briefly, remembering.

Then she said:

> "The first one said, **If your number is 18, don't go.**"
>
> "The second said, **If your number is 1, make sure she does.**"

Maya felt something cold move through her that had nothing to do with the rain.

"She?"

June nodded.

"It said *she*."

"You knew Candidate 1 was a woman?"

"No."

"You knew before coming here."

"I knew the post."

"That's why you weren't surprised."

"I was surprised."

"Less surprised."

"Fine. Less."

Maya took the book from her.

The damp ink on the back page had already dried.

**1 — ACTIVE**

**11 — ACTIVE**

**18 — NO RESPONSE**

**42 — PENDING**

**63 — PENDING**

Five numbers.

Five candidates.

One warning.

"Search for eighteen," Maya said.

June stared at her.

"We were literally told not to."

"Yes."

"And that doesn't bother you?"

"It bothers me enormously."

"Then why—"

Maya picked up her laptop.

"Because Professor Haruki said something today."

"What?"

Maya opened a browser.

"'I don't know' is becoming expensive."

June slowly smiled.

"That's terrible advice."

"Probably."

Maya typed:

**"18" "don't go"**

June sat beside her.

"Wait."

"What?"

"If whoever is doing this can see our searches—"

"They probably can."

"Then don't search what they told us not to search."

"Why?"

June reached over and deleted the query.

Then typed:

**missing student today Bay Area**

Maya looked at her.

June hit Enter.

"Different question," she said.

The results loaded.

Traffic accidents.

A runaway fourteen-year-old found safely.

A missing dog.

A campus notice.

June stopped scrolling.

Maya saw it too.

Posted forty-seven minutes earlier.

**STUDENT WELFARE NOTICE — DIABLO VALLEY COLLEGE**

A nineteen-year-old student had failed to return home.

Last known location:

a campus library.

Name:

**Noah Bennett.**

June clicked.

The photo showed a thin young man with curly hair and a tired smile.

Under the official notice, someone had already added a comment.

It had been posted twelve minutes earlier by an account with no profile picture.

Just two characters.

**18**

The comment contained one sentence.

> **I found the question.**

Maya stopped breathing.

The comment vanished.

Page refreshed.

Gone.

June whispered:

"Did you see that?"

"Yes."

Both phones vibrated.

This time the message was identical.

> **NOW YOU UNDERSTAND WHY WE TOLD YOU NOT TO SEARCH.**

A second line appeared.

> **TOMORROW. 7:00 PM.**

Then a third.

> **BRING THE BOOK.**

And finally:

> **COME WITHOUT PATH.**

Maya stared at the screen.

June was the first to speak.

"How exactly do you come without Path?"

Maya looked at the dark window.

Her own reflection looked back at her.

Behind it, reflected in the glass, was the municipal camera across the street.

Watching.

She closed her phone.

"I think," Maya said, "that's the first question we're supposed to solve ourselves."

**End of Chapter 2**
